Hourly Aerosol Optical Depth Retrieval

    Atmospheric aerosols are not only air pollutants that are harmful to human health, but they also play an important role in understanding climate change. As aerosol optical depth (AOD) provides a quantitative estimate of the aerosols in the atmosphere, it can be used as an indicator of air quality. Due to the limited spatial distribution of ground-based observation stations, continuous monitoring is challenging, prompting the widespread use of satellite-based AOD monitoring. Geostationary satellites are particularly advantageous for real-time and accurate monitoring of aerosols with high spatiotemporal variability. Monitoring aerosols with spatial variations in environmental factors can provide better understanding of the spatiotemporal patterns. Therefore, we aimed to accurately retrieve hourly AOD by integrating the use of geostationary satellites with environmental variables.


   Artificial intelligence techniques were employed to retrieve hourly AOD using various input features, evaluating the effectiveness of spatial information and identifying key variables driving AOD variability. GOCI geostationary satellite data were used to analyze the influence of spatiotemporal weather variations on AOD fluctuations. Furthermore, an algorithm utilizing hyperspectral data from the GEMS satellite was developed to extend AOD monitoring across broader regions.

Nighttime and High spatial resolution Aerosol Optical Depth Retrieval

    Currently, there is no operational satellite-based AOD monitoring that provides continuous full-time coverage over specific region at night. In East Asia, to monitor natural and anthropogenic emission spatiotemporally, it is necessary to develop the continuous AOD during both daytime and nighttime. Using the data-driven AOD estimation model enables us to monitor the transport of AOD distribution even at nighttime, implying the possibility to monitor transboundary pollution.

    AOD has been provided by MODIS, Aqua and Terra satellites at the spatial resolution of up to 1 km. However, Kang et al., 2023 developed the granular 250 m AOD product, by utilizing the spatial advantages of high revolutionized top of atmosphere reflectance and surface reflectance from MODIS aboard global satellite - Aqua and Terra satellites, using Light-GBM model. In addition, the Explainable AI-based tool, Shapley Additive explanation, is used for analyzing the variable contribution of the developed model by comparing the physics-based AOD retrieval.

Real-Time Learning algorithm

     While methods for estimating ground-level concentrations of major air pollutants such as PM, NO2, and O3 have been improved through machine learning and deep learning approaches, most studies still heavily rely on historical cumulative datasets. However, the use of such past data has the significant limitation of relatively low performance in predicting future air pollutant concentrations. The Real-Time Learning (RTL) approach has been introduced, which builds new models every hour based on the most recent datasets, allowing for the estimation of high-quality air pollutant concentrations.

    The ground-level air pollutant concentration data generated by this RTL approach are officially available on the NIER website (https://nesc.nier.go.kr/en/html/index.do) and are updated hourly, providing the most current and accurate air quality information for the public and researchers.


 

Particulate matter (PM) estimation

    The main indicator of PM estimation is satellite-based AOD. However, the satellite-based products have limitations for only providing the information under clear sky conditions. It indicates the limited spatial coverage to monitor ground-level PM estimates using satellite imagery. Therefore, Park et al., 2020 firstly generated the gap-filled AOD, and estimated PM10 and PM2.5 concentrations under all-sky conditions. It enables us to obtain spatially continuous PM distributions over East Asia. Moreover, not only for using machine learning approach, but also fusing machine learning and data assimilation, Lee et al. (2022) improved air quality forecast accuracy.


So far, most previous studies have used satellite-based AOD products to estimate ground-level PM2.5. However, the use of AOD products as an input required computationally intensive radiative transfer models and uncertainties in areas with high surface reflectance. By developing a machine learning-based model that directly estimates ground-level PM2.5 concentrations using TOAR from the GOCI-I/-II satellites instead of conventional AOD products, it was able to improve spatial coverage and estimation accuracy (Choi et al., 2023).
 

Gaseous substances estimation

    Gaseous pollutants like NO2 and O3 significantly impact air quality and human health by forming secondary pollutants and causing direct harm. Ground-level monitoring is essential, and AI was used to model the non-linear relationship between satellite vertical column density (VCD) and surface concentrations. By integrating land and ocean models, we developed a comprehensive system that monitors transboundary air pollution, extending coverage to marine areas.
   While satellite observations provide valuable insights into detailed atmospheric information, they are often affected by data gaps due to cloud cover. To address this issue, we developed an air pollution monitoring system that integrates a clear-sky model utilizing satellite data with a cloudy-sky model based on non-satellite data. This comprehensive approach allows for continuous air pollutant monitoring, even under cloudy conditions or at night.
   Further, the recent ongoing-study conduct advanced deep learning-based air pollutant development not only for single air pollutant but also multiple air pollutants, considering the complex interaction between air pollutants. Fusing the spatiotemporal characteristics of diverse data sources enabled us to estimate accurate full-coverage air pollutants over East Asia.
